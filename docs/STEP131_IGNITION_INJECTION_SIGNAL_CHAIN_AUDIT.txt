BUA / GM 1227165 STEP 131 - IGNITION/INJECTION SIGNAL-CHAIN AUDIT

Purpose
-------
Step 131 traces the visible ignition and injection signal chain around U11,
U12, bus-connected U9, and the executable firmware. It improves device
attribution and theory of operation without inventing custom-device internals.

Findings
--------
1. U9 (16045148), not the CPU itself, is the bus-connected custom peripheral
   whose visible pins include INJREF, IGNREF, ESTLOOP, KNOCK, IGN, INJS and
   INJA. The listing's $3FC0-$3FFF window is strongly attributable to U9 at the
   device level; exact pin-to-register mapping remains unresolved.

2. U12 visibly receives the conditioned REF path, CYL, IGN, EST, BYPASS and
   loop/limit inputs; it produces INJREF, IGNREF, IRQ, PUMP and ESTLOOP. A
   second drawn U12 section accepts INJS/INJA and current-sense signals and
   drives INJOUT/Q1. No undocumented logic or polarity is assigned.

3. The firmware consumes distributor-reference status at $3FFA, copies the
   period from $3FC0, reads $3FC8, and derives RPM at $CDE6-$CE41. This closes
   the supported processor-visible side of the reference path without claiming
   that one U9 input pin maps directly to a specific bit or register.

4. Spark timing is staged through $3FDC, $3FE4, $3FE6, $3FE8, $3FEC and $3FF6,
   with EST/bypass control through $3FFC. Error 42 uses feedback state and
   transition logic, but its precise electrical derivation from ESTLOOP is a
   strong inference rather than direct proof.

5. Injection service is gated by sampled-status bit $00A0 bit 6, distinct from
   reference bit 3. Normal synchronous pulse width is written to $3FD0;
   factory-test writes independently confirm $3FCE as an EFI delay register.
   Driver waveform, double-fire phase and current limiting remain custom-device
   boundaries.
