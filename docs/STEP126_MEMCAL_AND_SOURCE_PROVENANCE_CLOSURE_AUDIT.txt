1. 66-contact MEMCAL architecture clarified
--------------------------------------------

The MEMCAL/J4 interface is documented as 66 contacts total, with 33
corresponding positions on each side of the carrier.

The original reverse-engineering notes enumerated only contacts/positions 1-33.
The opposite half, contacts 34-66, was intended to be extrapolated from the
corresponding carrier positions. The absence of a separately written 34-66 list
must not be interpreted as evidence for a 33-contact MEMCAL.

The living architecture record is:

  docs/reference/MEMCAL_ARCHITECTURE.md

3. 16055375 reconstruction resolved
------------------------------------

The surviving evidence establishes 16055375 as a 14-terminal resistor network.
The physical replacement was implemented on a generic 16-position grid board.
The supported local position mapping is:

  terminals 1-7   -> J1-J7
  J8, J9          -> unused package positions
  terminals 8-14  -> J10-J16

The zero-ohm SMD parts are routing/configuration jumpers used to adapt the
common grid board to the required network connectivity. They are not calibration
resistor values.

A strong topology cross-check is that the LTspice model places terminals 7 and
8 on a common node while the KiCad reconstruction uses its zero-ohm routing to
make J7 and J10 common under the supported 14-to-16 position mapping.

The reconstruction record is:

  evidence/memcal/MEMCAL_16055375_RECONSTRUCTION_RECORD.md

Step 126 also tightened this record so that bare LTspice numeric values are not
silently represented as literal kOhm simulator values. The record now separates
the LTspice source numbers from the corresponding reconstructed physical kOhm
scale and explicitly documents the internal-node difference around the
100/220-element region. This prevents a component-for-component equivalence
claim that the evidence does not support.

4. 16055376 reconstruction resolved
------------------------------------

The surviving evidence establishes 16055376 as a 16-terminal network. Its
KiCad reconstruction exposes J1-J16 directly, so the local package-position
mapping is one-to-one.

The nominal LTspice/simple-network value family corresponds to the historical
Resistors.txt group:

  150, 1.5, 130, 24, 10, 8.2, 5.1, 7.5, 10

at the reconstructed physical kOhm scale.

The generic board again uses zero-ohm jumpers and deliberately open positions
for routing. Slight fitted-value substitutions in the KiCad reconstruction are
preserved as reconstruction choices rather than rewritten as proof of the
original molded network's exact internal values.

The reconstruction record is:

  evidence/memcal/MEMCAL_16055376_RECONSTRUCTION_RECORD.md

5. Historical research notes corroborate the network geometry
--------------------------------------------------------------

The user-maintained Usefull Info.pdf independently associates 16055375 and
16055376 with the relevant 1986-1988 5.0/5.7 TPI MEMCAL family and describes
them as seven-pins-per-side and eight-pins-per-side networks respectively.

That independently agrees with the 14-terminal and 16-terminal reconstruction
results.

The same notes preserve BUA / 9340 / 16059335 / 1227165 application information.
They are treated as historical corroboration rather than substituted for direct
PROM, schematic, listing, or physical evidence. Tentative replacement-part
language remains a research lead only.

6. Corrected BUA assembly-source provenance established
-------------------------------------------------------

A complete corrected build set was located under the project owner's Drive
ASM11 folder, including:

  bua-hac.asm
  bua-hac.lst
  bua-hac.s19
  bua-hac.bin
  BUA_from_Car.bin
  BUA 86 vette 5.7TPI auto with 3.07.bin

The Drive bua-hac.lst is byte-for-byte identical to the repository's existing
authoritative evidence/firmware/bua-hac.lst.

The corrected bua-hac.asm contains source lines 1-14724 corresponding to the
zero-error/zero-warning listing in order. The source/listing relationship is
therefore established rather than inferred from filename similarity.

The original Drive bua-hac.s19 and bua-hac.bin were compared to the listing:

  * all 16,092 explicitly emitted listing bytes agree with the S-record;
  * all 16,092 explicitly emitted listing bytes agree with the generated 16 KiB
    binary.

The verified build relationship is therefore:

  bua-hac.asm -> bua-hac.lst / bua-hac.s19 / bua-hac.bin

The authoritative listing remains the repository's primary emitted-address and
byte evidence, while the corrected .asm is now a verified source counterpart.

The detailed provenance record is:

  evidence/firmware/BUA_SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md

7. Corrected build versus historical PROM images
------------------------------------------------

The corrected generated bua-hac.bin differs from the historical
"BUA 86 vette 5.7TPI auto with 3.07.bin" at only:

  $C007  checksum byte
  $C6AE  documented corrected value ($B6 versus historical $84)

The source itself documents the $C6AE correction and resulting checksum change
from the older $9708 result to $973A.

BUA_from_Car.bin adds only one further difference at $C005, within the
sequence-number field.

This closes the earlier search for the original corrected source and generated
comparison artifacts. The historical build products survived and their
relationship to the authoritative listing is directly verified.

8. Repository preservation boundary
------------------------------------

The corrected .asm, original .s19, generated .bin, and historical PROM images
remain identified external Drive artifacts by exact filename, size, and
SHA-256 in the repository evidence records.

An attempted connector-based transfer of the S-record/binary into GitHub was
rejected after verification showed the staged content was not trustworthy as a
byte-preserving copy. Those temporary files were removed. Step 126 deliberately
prefers a verified external artifact with a recorded cryptographic hash over a
repository copy whose transfer fidelity cannot be proven.

Therefore no repository file should be treated as a replacement for the raw
Drive .asm/.s19/.bin unless it is later imported by a byte-preserving method
and its SHA-256 is verified against the values already recorded in
BUA_SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md.
