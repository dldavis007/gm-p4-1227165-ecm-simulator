Established shared inputs
-------------------------
The factory loop reads two FMD SPI reply bytes and repeatedly evaluates
diagnostic and battery A/D results. The authoritative listing uses the same
LF1E0/LF1E5 FMD byte-selection routines seen in normal operation. Steps 157 and
158 already established named raw VOLT, DIAG, and two-byte FMD backing.

Step 164 adds bua_lifecycle_irq_from_hal_step164(). When $0047 bit 7 selects
factory mode, the wrapper refreshes only these existing factory shadows:

  sim_factory_battery_adc117    <- U10 selector $10 / raw VOLT
  sim_factory_diagnostic_adc117 <- U10 selector $70 / raw DIAG
  sim_factory_fmd_byte1_117     <- normal raw FMD byte 1
  sim_factory_fmd_byte2_117     <- normal raw FMD byte 2

It then calls the unchanged Step-121 lifecycle IRQ entry. When factory mode is
not selected, the wrapper does not modify any factory shadow and the ordinary
scheduler runs normally.

Regression coverage
-------------------
The Step-164 regression contains ten checks. It proves:

- Step-163 raw startup enters the factory IRQ route;
- both FMD bytes, VOLT, and DIAG overwrite deliberately stale factory shadows;
- refreshed FMD mode and ignition-on VOLT drive the existing mode/timer state;
- factory IRQ routing remains isolated from ordinary IRQ tick accounting;
- raw DIAG 39 and 100 reach the $FDB3 diagnostic SWI before FMD exchange;
- raw VOLT 40 increments the factory ignition-off timer;
- raw VOLT 40 with timer 160 reaches the $FD03 powerdown SWI boundary;
- raw VOLT 89 preserves and equality 90 clears the timer;
- the normal IRQ route neither enters nor refreshes factory-only state.

Evidence boundary
-----------------
Step 164 does not model SPI electrical timing, FMD internal behavior, diagnostic
resistor voltages, battery scaling, power removal, watchdog hardware, or the
custom output exercises. SWI effects remain explicit processor/HAL boundaries.
The wrapper only removes duplicate PC stimulus for already-established raw
processor-facing inputs.

Conclusion
----------
Factory selection and continued factory IRQ execution now share one raw HAL
source for VOLT, DIAG, and both FMD bytes. Ordinary execution remains isolated,
and every factory threshold and SWI decision remains owned by the previously
translated executable behavior.
